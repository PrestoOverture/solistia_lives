# The Peculiar Librarian

> **Osvald & Temenos** · Montwise · ~2,094 words  
> Opening: *Osvald comes to town on scholarly business; Temenos is there between investigations.*  
> Written by Claude Opus 4.6, checked against the knowledge base by the validator.

The stairs to the second basement floor creaked under Osvald's weight. He adjusted his spectacles and counted: forty-three steps from the main level to the geology section, each one groaning in a predictable sequence. The Montwise Grand Library had not changed since his last visit, though the memories it held were hardly fond.

He turned the corner into the stacks. The smell of old paper and leather bindings filled the narrow corridor, and the light from the high windows barely reached this depth. Osvald ran a finger along the spines, reading titles in the dim light. *Mineral Composition of the Winterlands.* *Surveying the Crestlands Ridge.* He was looking for a specific treatise on crystalline lattice structures—relevant to Elena's lessons, whenever she was ready to resume them. Clarissa had written that Elena was recovering well, asking questions about mathematics again. The thought settled in his chest, warm and uncomfortable in equal measure.

His hand paused on a slim volume. *Formations of the Northern Traverse.* He pulled it free and flipped through the pages, scanning the diagrams. Adequate, though the author's methodology was sloppy. Still, it would serve as a primer.

"Well, well."

Osvald did not startle. He closed the book and turned.

Temenos Mistral leaned against the end of the bookshelf, arms folded, his white cape catching what little light filtered down from above. His expression was mild, amused in that quietly insufferable way he had.

"Temenos." Osvald tucked the book under one arm. "What brings you here?"

"Oh, the usual. Following a thread that may or may not lead anywhere." Temenos straightened and strolled closer, his staff tapping lightly on the stone floor. "A merchant in Canalbrine mentioned a suspicious shipment of relics passing through Montwise. I thought I'd see what the archives have to say about the provenance of certain antiquities. Dreadfully boring work, really." He tilted his head. "And you? Returning to the scene of your triumph, so to speak?"

Osvald grunted. He had no desire to linger on what had happened in the laboratory beneath their feet. The hidden passage was still there, two aisles over, sealed now by order of the town council. He had not asked what they had done with Harvey's chimeras. He did not want to know.

"I'm here for a book," he said. "Nothing more."

"Ah. A book." Temenos's smile widened slightly. "In a library. How thoroughly mundane."

Osvald met his gaze without expression. Temenos's needling rarely required a response. The cleric would either continue or he would not.

"Still," Temenos went on, "I suppose it's strange that we should both find ourselves here. You'd think after all that excitement, Montwise would be the last place either of us would choose to visit."

"The library has resources I need." Osvald glanced at the book in his hand, then back at Temenos. "My reasons are practical."

"Of course they are." Temenos paused, his gaze shifting past Osvald to the shadowed stacks beyond. "I visited here once before, you know. Years ago. Pontiff Jörg sent me to retrieve a theological manuscript. The librarian at the time was... an interesting individual."

"Interesting how?"

"I'm not entirely sure." Temenos frowned, a rare flicker of genuine puzzlement crossing his face. "He kept insisting that every book in the collection was alive in some metaphysical sense. That they dreamed when the library closed at night. He also claimed to be able to hear them whispering." He glanced at Osvald. "I wrote it off as eccentricity, but now I wonder. The church does have a habit of attracting people who see things others don't."

Osvald considered this. "Did you investigate?"

"I was young. I took the manuscript and left." Temenos's tone was light, but something underneath was not. "One of many threads I failed to follow at the time. Jörg knew, I think. He sent me here deliberately, but I didn't ask why."

Osvald studied him. Temenos had a way of appearing idle while his mind worked furiously beneath the surface, cataloging details, weighing motives. It was not so different from Osvald's own habit of internal debate, though Temenos hid it better.

"You're wondering if the librarian had a connection to the Order," Osvald said.

"Perhaps. Or perhaps I'm simply looking for patterns where none exist." Temenos shrugged. "Doubt is what I do, after all. It's hard to stop."

They stood in silence for a moment. Osvald shifted the book to his other arm. The basement felt colder than it should, and he caught himself calculating the rate of heat loss through the stone walls, the volume of air in the corridor, the time it would take for—

He stopped. Old habits.

"The current librarian might know," he said. "If your predecessor was peculiar enough, someone will have kept a record. Or a story."

Temenos brightened. "Now there's a thought. Shall we?"

Osvald hesitated. He had intended to check out the book and leave, perhaps stop by Regulus's workshop to ensure the man had not pawned his telescope again. Investigating long-dead librarians was not on his list of priorities.

But Temenos was already walking toward the stairs, clearly expecting him to follow.

Osvald sighed and fell into step beside him.

---

The head librarian was a thin, hawk-nosed woman with silver hair pulled into a severe bun. She looked up from her desk as they approached, her eyes sharp and assessing.

"Master Vanstein," she said, inclining her head. "It's been some time. I trust you've found what you were looking for?"

"Yes." Osvald set the book on the desk. "I'd like to borrow this."

She made a note in her ledger with meticulous care, then turned her attention to Temenos. "And you, cleric? How may I assist the Order today?"

"Oh, nothing official," Temenos said, his tone warm and self-deprecating. "Just a bit of curiosity. I visited this library several years ago, and the librarian at the time left quite an impression. I was wondering if you might remember him. He had a rather... unique perspective on books."

The woman's expression did not change, but Osvald caught the slight stiffening in her posture. Interesting.

"That would be Master Cornelius," she said. "He retired six years ago."

"Retired," Temenos echoed. "I see. And where might I find him now?"

She set down her pen. "Master Cornelius passed away four years ago. A fall from a ladder in the Unusual Tomes department. An accident."

The word hung in the air.

Temenos smiled. "How tragic. He must have been quite dedicated to his work, to be climbing ladders at his age."

"He was." The librarian's voice was flat. "He was also prone to... flights of fancy. The staff found him disorienting."

"Flights of fancy," Temenos repeated. "Such as hearing books whisper?"

Her gaze sharpened. "You know about that."

"He mentioned it when I visited." Temenos leaned on his staff, the picture of casual interest. "Did he ever say what they were whispering about?"

The woman was silent for a long moment. Then, with visible reluctance, she said, "He kept a journal. After his death, we archived it with the library's administrative records. It was... unsettling. References to shadows in the stacks, to patrons who came only at night and left no footprints. The governing board decided it was the product of an unwell mind."

"But you're not sure," Osvald said.

She looked at him, and for the first time, he saw unease in her eyes. "No," she admitted. "I'm not."

Temenos straightened. "Might we see the journal?"

"It's not available for public review."

"Of course not." Temenos's smile turned gentle. "But we're not the public, are we? Master Vanstein is a respected scholar, and I am an Inquisitor of the Sacred Flame. If there's something in that journal that suggests foul play, it would be my duty to investigate."

The librarian studied them both. Osvald could see the calculation behind her eyes: the risk of refusal versus the risk of revelation.

Finally, she rose. "Wait here."

She disappeared into the back office. Osvald glanced at Temenos.

"An accident," he said.

"Quite." Temenos's expression was unreadable. "Though I suppose people do fall from ladders all the time."

"Not in libraries. Ladders here have safety rails. I've used them."

"Observant of you."

Osvald adjusted his spectacles. "If this connects to the Moonshade Order—"

"Then it's one more thread they left behind." Temenos's voice was quiet. "One more person they silenced."

The librarian returned with a leather-bound journal, its cover worn and stained. She set it on the desk between them. "You may read it here," she said. "It does not leave this room."

Osvald opened the journal. The handwriting inside was cramped and spidery, growing more erratic as the pages progressed. The early entries were mundane: cataloging notes, acquisition records, complaints about patrons who mis-shelved books. Then, three years before the man's death, the tone shifted.

*The whispers grow louder. They speak of a shadow that walks the archives after dark. I have seen the footprints—or rather, the absence of them. The dust is disturbed, but there are no marks.*

Further down:

*A man came today asking for texts on the Lineage of Light. I told him we had none, which was the truth. He smiled and said he would return. I did not like his smile.*

Osvald felt a chill. He turned the page.

*I have found the hidden door. It leads down, far down. I dare not follow, but I hear sounds rising from below. Machinery. Voices. Something is being built beneath us, and the books know. They scream in their shelves.*

Temenos leaned over Osvald's shoulder, reading silently. When they reached the final entry, dated two weeks before Cornelius's death, they both stopped.

*I will tell the church. Pontiff Jörg will listen. He sent the young cleric before; he will understand. Tomorrow I leave for Flamechurch.*

Osvald closed the journal slowly.

"He never made it," Temenos said.

"No."

The librarian was watching them. "Do you believe him?" she asked. "About the shadows?"

Osvald thought of the chimeras in the underground laboratory, the abominations Harvey had crafted from stolen research and Lumina blood. He thought of the Moonshade Order's reach, of how many people had died to keep their secrets buried.

"Yes," he said.

Temenos nodded. "The Order silenced him. Jörg must have suspected—that's why he sent me here in the first place. To see if I would notice what Cornelius noticed." His expression hardened. "But I didn't. I took the manuscript and left."

"You were following orders," Osvald said.

"That's not good enough."

Osvald understood. Guilt was a variable that defied easy calculation. He had carried it himself for years, convinced he should have seen Harvey's betrayal sooner, should have protected Rita and Elena better. Logic said otherwise, but logic could not always silence the heart.

He turned to the librarian. "Keep this journal locked away. If anyone asks about it, tell them nothing."

She nodded, relief flickering across her face. "What will you do?"

"Nothing," Temenos said. "There's nothing left to do. The Order is broken. The people responsible are dead." He picked up his staff. "But at least now I know. Master Cornelius deserved that much."

They left the library together, stepping out into the afternoon light. The streets of Montwise bustled with scholars and merchants, the ordinary noise of an academic town. Osvald blinked, adjusting to the brightness.

"Thank you," Temenos said.

Osvald glanced at him. "For what?"

"For staying. For helping." Temenos's smile was faint. "You could have walked away."

Osvald shrugged. "It was logical. You needed confirmation, and I had the knowledge to provide it."

"Hmm. Is that all it was?"

Osvald did not answer. He thought of Jörg, a man he had never met but whose death had set so much in motion. He thought of Cornelius, who had seen the truth and died for it. He thought of all the people the Order had erased, whose stories would never be told.

"We march toward the dawn," he said finally. "That means all of us. Even the ones who fell along the way."

Temenos studied him for a long moment, then nodded. "Yes," he said. "I suppose it does."

They parted ways at the plaza. Osvald watched Temenos disappear into the crowd, then turned toward Regulus's workshop. The book on crystalline structures was tucked under his arm, and Elena's next lesson was waiting.

The shadow had passed. The dawn had come. But the names of the lost still mattered, and some debts could only be repaid with memory.

Osvald adjusted his spectacles and walked on.
