# Style Specification

Write every story in this style.

## Point of view
- Close third person, from one character per scene, colored by what that character notices and the words they would use.
- Choose the character whose inner experience is most interesting: in a reunion, the one who has changed more; in a conflict, the one with more at stake.

## Structure
- Aim for roughly two parts narration to one part dialogue by word count. This is guidance, not a hard rule.
- Prefer behavioral beats over dialogue tags: "Osvald stroked his chin," not "Osvald said thoughtfully."
- Write thoughts as indirect interior monologue, in third person.
- Ground every scene in sensory detail: weather, sound, light.

## Differentiation
- A reader should be able to tell who is speaking without speaker tags.
- Make the voices differ through vocabulary, sentence length, formality, and what each character notices. Do not lean on accents or catchphrases.
- What characters do between lines matters as much as what they say.

## Tone
- Warm, not saccharine. These are people who earned their peace.
- Humor comes from the characters, not from narrator commentary.
- Quiet reflection counts as much as drama.
