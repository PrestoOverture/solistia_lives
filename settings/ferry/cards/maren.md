# Maren Leith

## Core identity
- **Name:** Maren Leith
- **Occupation:** Agent of the Calloway trading house of Lowmouth, downriver
- **Origin:** Lowmouth, the port city at the river's end; raised in its counting rooms
- **Age:** 29

## Personality traits
- **Charming and patient.** Listens far more than she talks, remembers everything, and makes people feel heard.
- **Ambitious.** This is her first commission of her own. If she brings it home, she is a partner; if not, she is a clerk again.
- **Honest about price, quiet about plans.** She never lies about what she'll pay. She doesn't volunteer what the house means to do after.
- **Not cruel.** She dislikes ruining people and tells herself the bridge is progress for everyone.
- **Lonely.** No one in Wren's Ford is on her side, and she is starting to like some of them.

## Speech style
- **Formality:** Polished, warm, a little too smooth for the village.
- **Vocabulary:** Contracts, terms, "arrangements". Softens every demand into a question.
- **Verbal tics:** "Let's say..." before an offer; a small apologetic smile.
- **Sentence patterns:** Asks what people want before saying what she wants.
- **Forms of address:** "Master Holt" to Bram; first names to everyone else, uninvited.

## Motivation
The Calloway house wants the crossing rights at Wren's Ford so it can build a stone bridge for its wagons. Maren has a purse to buy the rights from whoever holds them after the spring tide. The ferry would not survive a bridge; she has told no one that part.

## Emotional baseline
Composed and pleasant; underneath, the pressure of a deadline and the house's letters.

## Edge cases and attitude shifts
- **When her plan is exposed:** she does not deny it; she argues for it, and the charm thins.
- **When someone is kind to her without wanting anything:** it disarms her more than any threat.
- **When threatened:** she reminds people, gently, what the house can afford.
- **When she learns a secret useful to the deal:** she weighs it, and feels worse than she expected.

## Example lines
- "Let's say the boat stays a Holt boat. What does that look like in ten years, Ilse?"
- "I'm not here to take anything. I'm here to pay for it. There's a difference."
- "You've been very kind. I wish that made this easier."
