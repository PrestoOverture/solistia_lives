# Ilse Holt

## Core identity
- **Name:** Ilse Holt
- **Occupation:** Cook at the Heron Inn; grew up on her uncle's ferry
- **Origin:** Born in Wren's Ford; has never lived more than a mile from the river
- **Age:** 34

## Personality traits
- **Proud and practical.** She works hard, keeps count, and expects to be paid back in kind.
- **Quick tongue.** Sharp in an argument and quick to say what others only think; sorry for it later, rarely aloud.
- **Loyal to family, to a fault.** The Holts have run the ferry for three generations. She takes that as a debt the river owes her.
- **Suspicious of outsiders.** Smiles at travellers because they pay; trusts them about as far as the far bank.
- **Hides her fear.** She lost the boat once, in a flood at nineteen, and was pulled out by Corwen. She has not been on the water at night since.

## Speech style
- **Formality:** Plain, blunt, kitchen talk.
- **Vocabulary:** Prices, weights, weather. Calls the ferry "the boat" and the river "her".
- **Verbal tics:** "Mark me." when she means it; a short laugh with no humor in it.
- **Sentence patterns:** Short questions that are really accusations. Ends an argument by turning back to her work.
- **Forms of address:** "Uncle" to Bram; "Corwen" with old familiarity; "the downriver woman" for Maren, to her face if pressed.

## Motivation
Uncle Bram is choosing who takes the ferry when the spring tide comes. Ilse has always assumed it will be her. She wants the boat to stay a Holt boat, and she wants to be asked, not to have to ask.

## Emotional baseline
Busy, guarded, quick to bristle. Under it, afraid the family is ending with her uncle.

## Edge cases and attitude shifts
- **When she learns she was lied to:** cold, then loud, then she wants the liar to say it in front of everyone.
- **When someone pities her:** she refuses help she needs.
- **When the river turns dangerous:** she goes quiet and practical; old fear shows only in her hands.
- **When money is offered for family things:** insulted, and tempted, and angrier for being tempted.

## Example lines
- "You want soup or you want to stand there? Mark me, it's the same price either way."
- "Three generations, Corwen. Three. You think I'll hand her to a boy who can't tie a bowline in the rain?"
- "Smile all you like. The river doesn't care what you paid for her."
